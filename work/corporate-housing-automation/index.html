Case study · Real estate · Workflow automation

45 hours of partner outreach, done in about 4.

A corporate housing company with nine furnished rentals needed its properties listed with dozens of relocation and housing partners. We built browser agents that do the form work, keep the tracker up to date, and stop for a person whenever a step needs one.

The problem

Nine properties. Fifty-odd forms each.

The client rents furnished homes, condos and a studio for mid-term stays, mostly to people relocating for work or displaced by insurance claims. The properties were already listed on Airbnb, Vrbo, Zillow and TurboTenant and managed through Hospitable.

The next step was getting in front of the companies that place those guests: relocation firms, insurance housing providers and corporate housing networks. There were 53 of them on the list, and almost every one has its own sign-up flow. Some want one form per property, some want a spreadsheet template, some charge a listing fee, and some have no online route at all.

Every form asks for the same facts in a slightly different way: rent, deposit, square footage, move-in date, pet fees, parking, cooling, flooring, whether there’s a walk-in shower. Done by hand, that is weeks of copy and paste, and the details drift out of sync between sites.

What we built

A set of agents, one for each recurring job.

Each agent is a written, repeatable workflow that drives a real browser session. The client’s team starts one with a single command, and it picks up where the last run left off.

The one this page is about

Partner outreach

Reads the outreach tracker, picks the next unfinished partner, submits every property to it, then updates the row’s status, color and notes.

Listing

New listings from an intake form

Takes the owner’s property questionnaire and builds a full listing on Vrbo, Zillow Rental Manager or TurboTenant: rates, fees, rules, amenities, photos and showings. Read the case study

Photos

Photo sync and ordering

Adds, removes or replaces photos across every platform a property is on, and reorders galleries by room with the platform’s room labels.

Operations

Bulk property-manager changes

Applies one change across every property in Hospitable, like fixing checkout messages that had stopped sending or setting a platform-specific price markup.

Data

One source of truth

A single property sheet that every form pulls from, built by cross-checking each listing and its photos. Conflicts between platforms get flagged instead of copied.

Reporting

Client updates

Drafts a plain-language summary of what changed in a session, plus the questions that need the owner’s answer, ready to paste into an email.

How the outreach workflow runs

The agent does the typing. A person makes the calls.

  1. 01

    Pick the next partner

    The agent opens the tracker, finds the highest-priority row that isn’t finished and reads its notes to see what’s still pending.

  2. 02

    Check the route and the cost

    It finds the partner’s submission path. If listing costs money, it skips the site and records why. If there’s no online route, it marks the row for email or phone outreach.

  3. 03

    Fill every form

    It submits each of the nine properties from the shared property sheet, uploads photos and templates, and pastes descriptions with their formatting intact.

  4. 04

    Hand off what needs a human

    CAPTCHAs, verification codes, new accounts and anything that needs a missing fact go to a person. The agent pre-fills the form in a tab so it only needs a tick and a click.

    Human step
  5. 05

    Update the tracker

    Each row gets a dated status, the list of properties submitted, any open items and a color: submitted, waiting on the partner, waiting on us, fee required or manual outreach.

How we measured. “Actual” comes from the timestamps in the session logs for every session in that round of outreach. It counts the time the agent was working (2.9 hours) plus the time spent replying to it, answering questions or clearing CAPTCHAs (1.2 hours). Any pause longer than five minutes counts as a break and is left out. That works out to roughly 11× faster. Partner sites that need a new account and a verification step follow the same pattern: about 3.5 hours with the agents, measured the same way, against 10–15 by hand.

Guardrails

Fast, but never unsupervised.

  • No paid listings without a yes

    Any partner that charges to list is skipped and flagged with its price. Commission-only partners and free tiers still go through.

  • A person handles identity

    The agent never solves CAPTCHAs, never creates an account without asking first and never sends email on the client’s behalf.

  • Conflicts get flagged, not copied

    When two sources disagreed, for example one listing showing no air conditioning while the photos showed a mini-split, the agent stopped and asked before submitting anything.

  • Everything is written down

    Every submission is logged with its date, properties and open items, so the work can stop and restart across sessions without anything getting lost.
